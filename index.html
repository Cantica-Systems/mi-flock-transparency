Michigan Flock transparency ledger · built from the public archive · day-by-day timeline

A plate read here is searchable from many states.

Every police agency running Flock Safety cameras publishes a transparency portal with a rolling thirty-day file: how many cameras it operates, every search its officers ran in that window, and the full list of outside agencies it has granted access to its data. The window moves every day, and what leaves it leaves the portal. This ledger captures that file from every available Michigan portal and keeps what the portals drop.

Source: the public Flock Safety transparency portals for the agencies in the archive, captured daily and committed to an append-only archive. Each portal shows about thirty days; the ledger keeps every capture. Figures here are counted from that archive, not estimated. Counties are counted separately throughout: an organization reachable from several counties is one organization in each, so county columns never sum to the combined figure.

FRESHNESS

How current each department’s data is

The ledger reads every portal once a day. For each department: when it was last read, how many searches that read added, when it last had anything new to add, and the date the portal itself printed. A department that has fallen behind the others is marked.

THE LATEST READ

What changed since the day before

Each day’s read is compared with the one before it: searches that are new, organizations added to or dropped from a sharing list, and organizations that left several portals at once. The timeline shows every day this way.

EACH DEPARTMENT’S CHOICE

Same cameras, opposite decisions

Sharing is not a Flock default. Each department decides for itself who may query its cameras, and neighbouring departments have decided very differently. Each county’s bar is the combined list of its departments, with no organization counted twice.

ONE WAY OR BOTH

Access runs one way more often than both

A department publishes two lists: who may search its cameras, and whose cameras it may search. Setting the two against each other shows that most of these relationships are not mutual: an outside agency can usually see in without the local department seeing out.

ON THE GROUND

The count they publish, and the cameras on the map

Each portal states how many cameras the department runs. Volunteers map cameras on OpenStreetMap, whoever runs them. The two rarely agree, and the notes say which way each department’s gap runs and why. One frame per county; every dot is a mapped camera and the outline is the boundary the department polices.

WHAT THEY FLAG

How often a passing car matches a hotlist

Each portal publishes how many vehicles its cameras saw in the last thirty days and how many of those matched a hotlist. One divided by the other is a rate that can be compared between departments, where the raw counts cannot.

        COMINGS AND GOINGS

        Who was added, who was dropped

        A live portal shows today’s list and nothing else. Every addition and removal here comes from comparing one day’s read with the next, which is the only place these changes can be seen.

        Organizations that left several portals at once

        One portal dropping a partner is a local decision. The same organization disappearing from several portals within two weeks is that organization leaving the network, and only reading every portal makes it visible.

        THE WHOLE NETWORK

        The network is shrinking, and the searches show it

        Every search also records how many camera networks it could reach. The widest search run on a given day measures the whole Flock network that day, and two departments with very different partners report nearly the same number, which is how we know it is the network being measured and not their own lists.

        What only the ledger can tell you

        Reading a portal today gives you a thirty-day window and a share list with no history. Reading the ledger gives you the same window plus every earlier one, and the difference between them. Partners appearing and disappearing, counters moving, a read that failed and recovered hours later: none of that is visible from the live page, by design.

        Caveats. State is inferred from the organization’s display name, since the portals publish no state field. Federal organizations are counted from the portal’s own [Federal] prefix. A county figure counts each organization once across the county’s departments, and counties do not add up to the combined figure, because most organizations can be reached from more than one.
