Overview

The Inkster Police Department (IPD) utilizes Flock Safety technology to support public safety and criminal investigations by providing objective, technology-based evidence while maintaining a strong commitment to individual privacy and constitutional rights.

IPD uses Flock Safety’s retroactive search capabilities to assist in solving crimes after they occur and utilizes real-time alerts for vehicles associated with active hotlists, helping officers identify and locate wanted vehicles and individuals.

To promote transparency, accountability, and responsible use, the Inkster Police Department has established policies and safeguards governing the use of Flock Safety technology. The Department also makes applicable usage policies and statistics available to the public.

Kids found safe after Inkster father duct tapes mom, flees with them to Ohio

The Brief

Two Inkster kids were found safe after being taken by their father and driven to Ohio.
The kids, ages 4 and 6, were recovered by police after calling for help. Their father allegedly shot himself.
He took the kids after duct taping their mom when he learned she had planned to end their relationship.

Timeline

Two kids were found safe in a cornfield in Norwalk, Ohio in Huron County after being found by police following an investigation that crossed state borders.

Inkster Police Chief Tameka Jenkins identified the suspect as the kids' father, who restrained their mother with duct tape shortly before 9 a.m. on Oct. 14.

He then took the kids and fled in the victim's vehicle.

After the victim freed herself, she called the suspect who told her they were in Rouge Park and threatened to kill himself. A similar call was made between the suspect and his grandmother, who reported it to Detroit Police.

Police used Flock cameras to determine the suspect was no longer in Inkster, Jenkins said during a Wednesday press conference.

Police eventually determined the suspect's location by tracking a cell phone, leading them to Ohio.

After making contact with Ohio police, they located the kids who were found unharmed.

Policy & Trust

What's Detected

License Plate Recognition (LPR) cameras capture and analyze vehicle license plates as vehicles pass through designated areas. The technology may identify the license plate number, vehicle make, model, color, and other observable vehicle characteristics.

LPR systems can also generate alerts when a vehicle matches an authorized law-enforcement hotlist, such as a stolen vehicle, wanted vehicle, or vehicle associated with an active investigation.

LPR technology does not identify or track a person's identity based solely on the camera image. An alert is an investigative lead and must be independently verified by an officer before any enforcement action is taken.

What's Not Detected

LPR technology is focused on vehicles and license plates. It does not:

1. Identify a person's name or personal identity;
2. Recognize or identify a person's face;
3. Record conversations or capture audio;
4. Determine a person's race, ethnicity, religion, or other personal characteristics;
5. Determine a person's immigration or citizenship status;
6. Monitor a person's phone, text messages, or other personal communications;
7. Access personal information simply because a vehicle passes an LPR camera; or
8. Automatically establish that a person has committed a crime.

LPR technology provides vehicle-related information and investigative leads. Any alert or information generated by the system must be independently verified by law-enforcement personnel before enforcement action is taken.

Acceptable Use Policy

License Plate Reader (LPR) data is used solely for legitimate law-enforcement and public-safety purposes, including criminal investigations, stolen or wanted vehicles, missing or endangered persons, and developing investigative leads.

LPR data is owned and controlled by the Inkster Police Department and is never sold or used for commercial purposes. Access is limited to authorized personnel and may be audited to ensure compliance with Department policy and applicable law.

LPR data shall not be used for personal purposes, harassment, discrimination, retaliation, or to monitor individuals engaged in lawful constitutionally protected activities.

Prohibited Uses

LPR technology and data shall not be used for:

1. Immigration enforcement or immigration-related investigations;
2. General traffic enforcement or issuing traffic citations;
3. Harassment, intimidation, retaliation, or discrimination;
4. Targeting or monitoring individuals based solely on a protected characteristic, including race, color, sex, religion, national origin, or other protected status;
5. Monitoring lawful protests, demonstrations, political activity, religious activity, or other constitutionally protected conduct;
6. Personal use or searches conducted out of curiosity;
7. Monitoring family members, friends, neighbors, coworkers, or other individuals for non-law-enforcement purposes;
8. Personal investigations or disputes;
9. Commercial purposes or the sale of LPR data to third parties; or
10. Any purpose that violates federal or Michigan law, Department policy, or the individual's constitutional rights.

Access Policy

All system access requires a valid reason and is stored indefinitely.

Hotlist Policy

Hotlist hits are required to be human verified prior to action.

Myth vs. Fact
Myth

Flock has been hacked.

Fact

Flock's cloud environment has never been breached. Flock invests in a strong security posture, including adhering to NDAA, SOC2 (Type II), ISO 27001, ISO 27018 FedRAMP 20x, and Higher Education Community Vendor Assessment Tool (HECVAT) compliance. Additional security precautions include Role-Based Access Controls, data encrypted at-rest and in transit, required Multi-Factor Authentication, and regular penetration testing.

Myth

Flock sells and/or shares customer data.

Fact

Your community owns 100% of your data and it will never be sold. Only your agency decides who to share your data with, not Flock. Flock Safety does not publicly release agency search data. Flock does not operate or support any public-facing searchable database of law enforcement lookups, and we do not provide license plate search histories to websites or third parties without the agency's knowledge and authorization. Other agencies who you share footage with have been responding to FOIA requests and sharing this search data.

Myth

My data is fed into a national database.

Fact

Each agency controls access to its own data. Agencies may choose to share information through a broader network, but nothing is shared automatically. Participation is opt-in, governed by defined agreements, and limited to lawful investigative purposes.

Myth

Flock cameras use facial recognition.

Fact

Flock cameras do not use facial recognition technology. Searches are based on vehicle characteristics, and not identity. Flock cannot recognize, identify, or track individuals. It is purpose built for specific public safety investigations, not to monitor people.
