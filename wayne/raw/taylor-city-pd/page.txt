Overview

Taylor MI PD uses Flock Safety technology to capture objective evidence without compromising on individual privacy. Taylor MI PD utilizes retroactive search to solve crimes after they've occurred. Additionally, Taylor MI PD utilizes real time alerting of hotlist vehicles to capture wanted criminals. In an effort to ensure proper usage and guardrails are in place, they have made the below policies and usage statistics available to the public.

Acceptable Use Policy

Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR) technology shall be used solely for legitimate law enforcement and public safety purposes. Users shall only access ALPR data in connection with an authorized investigation, intelligence function, or public safety objective. Personal, political, or discriminatory use of the system is strictly prohibited. All system activity is subject to audit and review, and violations may result in disciplinary action, termination of access privileges, or criminal prosecution. Data is owned by Taylor MI PD and is never sold to 3rd parties.

Prohibited Uses

-Personal reasons or personal curiosity.
-Looking up vehicles belonging to family members, friends, neighbors, coworkers, acquaintances, or romantic interests unless directly related to an authorized investigation.
-Conducting searches on themselves or their own vehicles.
-Monitoring elected officials, celebrities, public figures, or other individuals without a legitimate law enforcement purpose.
-Supporting political, religious, or ideological activities.
-Harassing, intimidating, stalking, or retaliating against any person.
-Discriminating against any individual based on race, ethnicity, national origin, religion, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, disability, or other protected status.
-Investigating individuals solely because they are exercising constitutional rights, including freedom of speech, assembly, religion, or association.
-Accessing ALPR information without an official law enforcement or public safety purpose.
-Sharing ALPR data with unauthorized persons or organizations.
-Using another employee's login credentials or allowing others to use their credentials.
-Circumventing audit requirements, retention policies, or access controls.
-Downloading, retaining, or disseminating ALPR data outside approved systems and procedures.
-Conducting fishing expeditions or broad searches without a specific law enforcement objective.
-Using ALPR information for commercial purposes or personal financial gain.
-Searching for information on current or prospective employees unless authorized as part of an official background investigation.

-Immigration enforcement, traffic enforcement, harassment or intimidation, usage based solely on a protected class (i.e. race, sex, religion), Personal use.

Access Policy

Authorized Users

Access to the ALPR system shall be limited to personnel who:

Have a legitimate law enforcement or public safety need.
Have completed required training.
Have been approved by the Chief of Police or designee.
Possess unique user credentials.
Access Requirements

Users may access the system only when:

Performing official duties.
Supporting a criminal investigation, intelligence function, traffic safety initiative, missing person investigation, or other legitimate public safety purpose.
Conducting activities authorized by department policy and applicable law.

Each search should be associated with:

A case report number;
CAD/event number;
Intelligence file number; or
Other documented law enforcement purpose.
User Responsibilities

Users shall:

Protect login credentials and not share passwords.
Ensure searches are accurate, relevant, and related to official duties.
Comply with all department policies regarding privacy, security, and data handling.
Report suspected misuse, unauthorized access, or security incidents.

Hotlist Policy

Investigative Hot List Entries

Vehicles may be entered into the ALPR hot list when they are reasonably believed to be associated with an active criminal investigation and when the entry serves a legitimate law enforcement purpose. Investigative entries must be supported by a documented case number, intelligence file, or other official investigative record.

Examples include, but are not limited to:

Narcotics trafficking investigations.
Violent crime investigations.
Robbery investigations.
Homicide investigations.
Human trafficking investigations.
Organized criminal activity investigations.
Fugitive apprehension efforts.


Investigative hot list entries shall be reviewed periodically and removed when the investigative need no longer exists.

Myth vs. Fact
Myth

Flock has been hacked.

Fact

Flock's cloud environment has never been breached. Flock invests in a strong security posture, including adhering to NDAA, SOC2 (Type II), ISO 27001, ISO 27018 FedRAMP 20x, and Higher Education Community Vendor Assessment Tool (HECVAT) compliance. Additional security precautions include Role-Based Access Controls, data encrypted at-rest and in transit, required Multi-Factor Authentication, and regular penetration testing.

Myth

Flock sells and/or shares customer data.

Fact

Your community owns 100% of your data and it will never be sold. Only your agency decides who to share your data with, not Flock. Flock Safety does not publicly release agency search data. Flock does not operate or support any public-facing searchable database of law enforcement lookups, and we do not provide license plate search histories to websites or third parties without the agency's knowledge and authorization. Other agencies who you share footage with have been responding to FOIA requests and sharing this search data.

Myth

My data is fed into a national database.

Fact

Each agency controls access to its own data. Agencies may choose to share information through a broader network, but nothing is shared automatically. Participation is opt-in, governed by defined agreements, and limited to lawful investigative purposes.

Myth

Flock cameras use facial recognition.

Fact

Flock cameras do not use facial recognition technology. Searches are based on vehicle characteristics, and not identity. Flock cannot recognize, identify, or track individuals. It is purpose built for specific public safety investigations, not to monitor people.
