Overview

Wyoming PD uses Flock Safety Automated License Plate Reader (ALPR) technology to capture objective evidence without compromising individual privacy. Wyoming PD uses retroactive searches to solve crimes after they occur or locate missing persons. Additionally, Wyoming PD utilizes real-time alerting of hotlist vehicles to capture wanted criminals. To ensure proper use and guardrails are in place, they have made the policies and usage statistics below available to the public.

MICHIGAN SUCCESS STORIES IN THE NEWS
Featured Success Stories

In addition to recovering stolen vehicles and license plates and arresting wanted individuals every year, the Flock Safety ALPR program has provided investigative leads in numerous police investigations, many of which would have gone unsolved or taken much more time and resources to solve. Below are just some of the many great successes the Wyoming Police Department has had utilizing the Flock Safety program.

• Homicide Investigations: Since 2022, ALPRs have played a critical role in solving homicide investigations. In July of 2026, the Wyoming Police Department responded to a homicide in which the victim's vehicle had been stolen. Within four hours, the vehicle and suspect were identified via an ALPR camera in another state. Local law enforcement partners located the suspect and took him into custody following a lengthy pursuit. Overall, homicides are down over 80% compared to five years ago, prior to LPR implementation.

• Arson Investigation: The victim’s vehicle was set on fire while in the parking lot of a local business. Surveillance cameras at the business showed the suspect fleeing the scene in a vehicle, and a search of ALPR cameras in the area provided investigators with a license plate number. An ex-boyfriend of the victim was charged with arson in connection with this case.

• Carjacking Vehicle Recovery: In 2024, a vehicle was stolen from a local business after the driver was assaulted and pulled from the vehicle. A 3-year-old child was inside at the time and was pushed out of the vehicle by the suspect further down the street. Using ALPR data and other investigative methods, officers located the vehicle and suspect in Chicago, IL, in less than 24 hours.

• Shooting Investigation: In 2025, a person was shot at an apartment complex by a neighbor, and the suspect fled the scene in a vehicle. Based only on the vehicle description provided by the victim, detectives identified the vehicle through the ALPR system. A search warrant was conducted at the suspect's apartment, where a stolen handgun and 1.1 kg of heroin/Carfentinal were located. The suspect was located and arrested for attempted murder, as well as other charges, based on this information.

• Sexual Assault Investigation: In 2025, a victim was violently sexually assaulted, requiring admission to the hospital. An ALPR alert resulted in detectives locating the suspect and conducting a search warrant of his residence. During the search, numerous firearms and illegal narcotics were located. The suspect was charged with felony sexual assault and assault with the intent to do great bodily harm.

• Property Crime and Theft: A series of larceny incidents occurred in several West Michigan counties in which over $100,000 in semi tires were stolen. ALPR data identified the suspect vehicles, leading to several arrests and the recovery of approximately $35,000 of the stolen tires.

• Endangered Missing Persons Located: ALPR alerts have allowed Wyoming PD officers to locate missing persons who suffered from dementia or had suicidal ideation.

• Auto Theft Reduction: FLOCK cameras have significantly improved the recovery of stolen vehicles and identification of suspects. Motor vehicle theft is down 60% compared to five years ago, prior to ALPR implementation. Additionally, 23% of vehicles stolen in Wyoming have been recovered due to the ALPR system.

Policy & Trust

What's Detected

License Plates, general vehicle characteristics

What Flock Does Not Do or Detect

• Facial recognition – Flock does not identify people by their faces
• Driver or passenger identification – It cannot tell who is a driver or passenger of a vehicle
• Record phone calls or conversations – There are no microphones for audio surveillance
• Read text messages or cell phone activity
• Track a person's phone location
• Recognize race, gender, or personal identity of vehicle occupants
• Predict crimes or automatically determine criminal activity
• Run every plate automatically through LEIN/NCIC
• Inside of homes or buildings


Acceptable Use Policy

Data is used for official law enforcement purposes only and is restricted to public safety-related missions of the agency. Only trained sworn law enforcement officers authorized by the police department administrator may access LPR systems. Data is owned by Wyoming PD and is never sold to 3rd parties.

Prohibited Uses

• Immigration enforcement
• Personal Use
• Traffic enforcement
• Harassment or intimidation of any individual or group, based solely on a protected class (i.e., race, religion, gender, ethnicity, nationality, sexual orientation, political affiliation, disability, or other classification protected by law)
• Identifying any individual or group in the exercise of First Amendment rights, such as lawful protests, unless used only for the protection of those persons

Access, Audit, and Search Policy

1) All system access requires a valid law enforcement reason.
2) All system searches require an offense type, police report number, and a reason entered before the search is allowed.
3) All system activity is routinely audited.
4) All system access is stored indefinitely.

Hotlist Policy

A hotlist is information from the National Crime Information Center (NCIC), Law Enforcement Information Network (LEIN), AMBER Alerts, or department-owned lists of vehicles wanted in association with a law enforcement investigation. Hotlist hits are required to be human verified prior to action.

Myth vs. Fact
Myth

Flock has been hacked.

Fact

Flock's cloud environment has never been breached. Flock invests in a strong security posture, including adhering to NDAA, SOC2 (Type II), ISO 27001, ISO 27018 FedRAMP 20x, and Higher Education Community Vendor Assessment Tool (HECVAT) compliance. Additional security precautions include Role-Based Access Controls, data encrypted at-rest and in transit, required Multi-Factor Authentication, and regular penetration testing.

Myth

Flock sells and/or shares customer data.

Fact

Your community owns 100% of your data and it will never be sold. Only your agency decides who to share your data with, not Flock. Flock Safety does not publicly release agency search data. Flock does not operate or support any public-facing searchable database of law enforcement lookups, and we do not provide license plate search histories to websites or third parties without the agency's knowledge and authorization. Other agencies who you share footage with have been responding to FOIA requests and sharing this search data.

Myth

My data is fed into a national database.

Fact

Each agency controls access to its own data. Agencies may choose to share information through a broader network, but nothing is shared automatically. Participation is opt-in, governed by defined agreements, and limited to lawful investigative purposes.

Myth

Flock cameras use facial recognition.

Fact

Flock cameras do not use facial recognition technology. Searches are based on vehicle characteristics, and not identity. Flock cannot recognize, identify, or track individuals. It is purpose built for specific public safety investigations, not to monitor people.
