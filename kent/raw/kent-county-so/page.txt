Overview

The Kent County Sheriff's Office (KCSO) employs Flock Safety's automated license plate recognition (ALPR) technology as a targeted investigative and public safety tool. ALPR delivers objective observations of vehicles, which can lead to investigative breakthroughs through retrospective searches after incidents are reported. It also offers real-time alerts for stolen vehicles or license plates, vehicles linked to wanted persons, missing or endangered individuals, and other critical public safety issues.

Since the implementation of ALPR technology, KCSO has experienced a significant positive impact on investigations and overall crime reduction efforts. Leads generated by ALPR have been instrumental in identifying and halting criminal activity, recovering stolen vehicles and property, supporting investigations into millions of dollars in reported losses, solving cases that might otherwise have remained unresolved, and locating missing or endangered individuals in Kent County, across Michigan, and throughout the surrounding region.

Criminal activities, stolen vehicles, and missing persons cases often cross jurisdictional lines. Therefore, responsible sharing of information with authorized law enforcement partners enables agencies to connect evidence, identify related criminal activities, and respond more swiftly in urgent situations.

KCSO utilizes ALPR technology with clearly defined safeguards. Searches must be linked to a valid criminal complaint number or a documented public safety emergency. All system activity is logged and reviewed by supervisors, and an audit process is in place. KCSO does not use ALPR information for civil immigration enforcement purposes.

This transparency portal offers the public access to KCSO's ALPR policies, including safeguards, data retention practices, and usage statistics. Our goal is to showcase the public safety benefits of this technology while also ensuring accountability in its usage.

What ALPR Captures, How Information Is Retained, and What an Observation Does Not Establish

The Kent County Sheriff’s Office uses automated license plate recognition (ALPR) technology to capture vehicle-related information from vehicles passing by an ALPR camera.

An ALPR observation may include:

- The license plate number
- An image of the vehicle and the license plate
- The date and time of the observation
- The location of the ALPR camera
- General visible vehicle characteristics, such as make, color, body style, and other distinguishing features

ALPR technology provides a time and location-based observation of a vehicle. It does not independently identify the driver or passengers of the vehicle, nor does it establish that a crime has occurred or that the vehicle or its occupants are involved in any criminal activity.

An ALPR alert or search result serves as an investigative lead. It must be evaluated alongside other available information and verified independently before any enforcement action is taken. Personnel must ensure they have the appropriate lawful basis for any investigative detention, search, arrest, or other law enforcement actions.

The standard retention period for ALPR observation data at KCSO is 30 days. Information may be retained beyond this period only if it is relevant to an authorized investigation, preserved as evidence, required by court order, or mandated by law. Evidence that is preserved is maintained in accordance with applicable evidence retention, records retention, and legal requirements.

Access to ALPR information is restricted to trained and authorized personnel. Searches and user activities are logged and may be reviewed to ensure compliance with KCSO policy, applicable laws, and authorized law enforcement purposes.

Authorized Purposes, Search Documentation, Information Sharing, and Oversight

As of September 2026, the Kent County Sheriff’s Office (KCSO) employs a network of 62 Flock automated license plate recognition (ALPR) cameras as a focused investigative tool for specific, legitimate law enforcement and public safety purposes.

KCSO personnel may access or search ALPR information only when it relates to their official duties and is supported by a specific, articulable law enforcement or public safety purpose.

Each search must be linked to a valid criminal complaint number or a documented emergent public safety need. If there is an immediate threat, a missing or endangered person, or another urgent circumstance that necessitates a search before a criminal complaint number can reasonably be obtained, personnel must document the public safety basis for the search. This documentation must be associated with the appropriate complaint, incident, or event record as soon as practicable.

Authorized uses of ALPR information may include:

- Investigating reported criminal activity
- Identifying or locating vehicles related to a criminal investigation
- Locating stolen vehicles or stolen license plates
- Assisting in the identification or recovery of stolen property
- Locating vehicles associated with individuals wanted for criminal activity or with valid arrest warrants
- Assisting in locating missing, endangered, or abducted persons
- Responding to serious or imminent threats to public safety
- Developing, confirming, or corroborating investigative leads
- Identifying possible connections between related criminal incidents
- Supporting authorized investigations conducted with other law enforcement or criminal justice agencies
- Other lawful criminal justice purposes as authorized by KCSO policy

ALPR information is intended to provide an investigative lead and should be considered alongside other available evidence. Merely observing a vehicle does not establish that a crime has occurred, identify who was driving or occupying the vehicle, or prove that the vehicle or its occupants were involved in criminal activity.

Before taking enforcement action based on a real-time alert, personnel must independently verify the observed license plate and vehicle information. They must also confirm that the underlying alert or record remains current and valid and establish an independent lawful basis for any actions taken. An ALPR alert does not replace the legal requirements for investigative detention, search, arrest, or other law enforcement actions.

Access to the ALPR system is restricted to trained and authorized personnel, with access levels appropriate to their official responsibilities. User activity, including searches, alerts, and information-sharing actions, is logged and may be reviewed or audited by supervisory personnel.

ALPR information may only be shared with authorized law enforcement or criminal justice partners for legitimate investigative or public safety purposes. Requests from other agencies must be linked to a valid criminal complaint, case number, or documented emergent public safety need. Information obtained through KCSO's ALPR system may not be used or shared further for unrelated or unauthorized purposes.

Responsible information sharing is crucial because criminal activity, stolen-vehicle cases, and missing-person investigations often span multiple jurisdictions. All information sharing must comply with KCSO policy, applicable laws, and established access and auditing requirements.

KCSO does not utilize or share ALPR information for civil immigration enforcement purposes.

Privacy, Civil Rights, and Civil Liberties Protections

The Kent County Sheriff's Office (KCSO) allows the use of Automatic License Plate Recognition (ALPR) information only for legitimate law enforcement and public safety purposes related to official duties.

KCSO personnel must not access, search, use, or share ALPR information for the following reasons:

- Curiosity, personal reasons, or any purposes unrelated to official duties.
- Harassment, intimidation, retaliation, or personal financial gain.
- Commercial purposes or for the benefit of unauthorized individuals, businesses, or organizations.
- Merely to determine where a person or vehicle has been observed without a legitimate law enforcement or public safety purpose.
- To broadly monitor or create a history of a person’s or vehicle’s movements without a specific, articulable, and authorized law enforcement purpose.
- To target individuals or groups based solely on race, ethnicity, national origin, religion, sex, gender, political beliefs or affiliations, disability, or any other characteristic protected by law.
- To identify, monitor, or collect information about individuals or groups solely because of their participation in lawful speech, assembly, association, worship, protest, political activity, or other First Amendment-protected activities.
- For civil immigration enforcement, including locating a person solely based on their actual or suspected civil immigration status.
- For any purpose prohibited by law, court order, or KCSO policy.
- With unauthorized individuals, organizations, or agencies.

These restrictions do not prevent KCSO from investigating reported criminal conduct, threats of violence, or other unlawful activities, even if such conduct occurs at or near a public gathering or is associated with an individual engaged in protected activities. Any ALPR search must be based on reported conduct, investigative facts, or public safety concerns, and not solely on a person’s beliefs, associations, protected characteristics, or participation in lawful activities.

The prohibition on civil immigration enforcement does not prevent KCSO from responding to an immediate safety threat or conducting a lawful criminal investigation within its authority.

Any suspected misuse of ALPR information will be subject to supervisory review. Confirmed misuse may lead to removal of system access, administrative investigation, disciplinary action, referral for criminal investigation (when appropriate), or other actions consistent with KCSO policy and applicable law.

Flock cameras do NOT:

The Kent County Sheriff's Office uses ALPR technology to identify vehicle-related information, not the identity or personal characteristics of the people inside a vehicle. The ALPR system does not use facial recognition, does not collect biometric information, and does not identify the driver or occupants of a vehicle. It does not determine a person's race, gender, identity, or whether an individual has committed a crime. The system provides vehicle-related information that may assist investigators in developing or confirming investigative leads; any determination regarding a person's identity or involvement in an incident requires additional investigative information and appropriate verification.

Flock cameras do NOT:
Detect, log, or connect to cell phones, airpods, vehicles, or any other consumer electronic device
Read text messages, emails, or intercept audio telephone calls
Detect speed or be used for speed enforcement
Detect or enforce red light violations or stop sign violations
Capture any kind of audio whether inside or outside of a vehicle

Alert Policy

A Flock alert is an investigative lead only and does not, by itself, establish reasonable suspicion or probable cause that a crime was committed. Deputies do not initiate enforcement action based solely on a Flock alert. Flock information may be used in conjunction with other investigative resources, observations, and information to establish an independent lawful basis for enforcement action.
